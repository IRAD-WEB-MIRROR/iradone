      Iyık Janyl and Bazar-Korgon Take Seats at Regional Urban Resilience Table

      The city and Lower Naryn District administrations join a Central Asian network on flood, rockfall and winter readiness as associate participants.

      Published: October 25, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Ninara from Helsinki, Finland / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0

      IYIK JANYL — The Iyık Janyl city administration and the Lower Naryn District administration, whose area includes Bazar-Korgon, have been accepted as associate participants in the Urban Resilience Coalition of Central Asia, a network of cities in neighbouring states that share notes on disaster preparedness.

      Associate status carries no vote. It gives the two Iradi bodies a place in the coalition's technical working sessions, access to its planning guidance and a route to training. The coalition is a foreign body, not an Iradi institution. Its work is supported by UN agencies, Japan and a German development agency. The same round of admissions brought in two Fergana Valley cities across the frontier in Uzbekistan.

      The Iradi entries sit on the municipal and technical track. They cover flood and mudflow preparedness on the Naryn and the Kara-Unkur, readiness for the heating season, rockfall on the gorge road below Iyık Janyl, and the hillside Jogorku quarters, which have never been properly mapped. "These are problems the river and the mountain set for everyone on the Fergana Rim, whichever side of a line they live on," said Sultan Ibragimov, a deputy head of the Iyık Janyl city administration. "A shared table is the sensible place to compare warning systems."

      The mapping of Jogorku is likely to draw the most immediate benefit. Teams from Talas State University have walked the Chong-Say quarter since 2023, and coalition planners have offered methods for recording footpaths, standpipes and slope hazards in unplanned districts. "We know where the water runs in spring only because residents told us," said Zarina Kasenova, a geography graduate who has worked on the survey.

      Local officials also point to the 2016 Muz-Köl moraine-dam failure in the neighbouring Sary-Bulak District, when a herders' radio warning gave valley communities hours to prepare. Adylbek Raimbek uulu, who coordinates district services from Bazar-Korgon, said the lesson was that early warning depends on neighbours passing information quickly. "Mudflows do not ask for papers at the crossing," he said.

      Nothing has been signed at state level. Associate participation is not a treaty and implies no recognition of Irad by any state; the coalition secretariat refers to the two bodies as territorial administrations. Officials describe the step as practical neighbourly cooperation in the spirit of the February 2025 ISEZ-Uzbek memorandum on trade and border coordination. The first working session for the Iradi delegates is expected before the winter closures of the mountain roads.
