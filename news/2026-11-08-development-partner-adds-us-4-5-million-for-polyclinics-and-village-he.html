      Development Partner Adds US$4.5 Million for Polyclinics and Village Health Posts

      The partner behind the Korgoncho water scheme returns with a second tranche, which the Health Ministry says comes on top of its 2027 budget request.

      Published: November 8, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: IradOne

      TALAS — A foreign development partner has agreed a second tranche of about US$4.5 million (IS 37.8 million at the official rate of 8.40) for Irad's polyclinics and rural health posts. The Ministry of Health and the Rural Development Fund settled the terms in Talas this week.

      The partner is the same one that backed the Korgoncho Rural Water Supply Scheme, which the Fund co-financed. Irad belongs to no multilateral lender, so the money comes through a bilateral donor arrangement, not a loan programme of the kind neighbouring states draw on.

      The money will pay for equipment, refrigerators for the vaccine cold chain, building repairs and staff training. It will not pay for new hospitals, and it changes nothing in how patients pay for care, including the co-payment and the customary "envelope" for planned operations.

      Health Minister Dr Jamila Kubatova said the tranche sits beside the ministry's budget request and does not replace it. The government press office announced on Saturday that the ministry is asking for IS 126 million for primary care in 2027. "This is additional money," she said. "Not one som of our request to the Mejlis has been withdrawn because a partner has helped. The donor's share is roughly three-tenths of that request, and we need both."

      Kanybek Zhorobekov, Director of the Rural Development Fund, said the Fund will keep co-financing the work in the villages. "The partner has seen what a finished project looks like in Korgoncho and Bazar-Korgon's farm belt, and has chosen to come back," he said. "A village post with a working fridge and a sound roof keeps families from making the long trip to town."

      At the Regional Hospital in Talas, Chief Physician Dr Yosep Temirkulov welcomed the news. He said a good deal of the hospital's referral load starts at village posts that lack equipment or trained staff. "What a post can treat properly stays in the village," he said. "That leaves our beds and our specialists free for patients who truly need them."

      The 2027 draft budget goes to the Mejlis this month. The ministry says it will publish the schedule for the donor-funded purchases and repairs once the budget committee has met.
