      Talas Dealer Plans US$3 Million Farm-Machinery Service Centre at Kara-Bulak

      Alatau Agro Machinery, backed by Talasbank and the Rural Development Fund, aims to open repair bays and a parts warehouse before the 2027 spring sowing.

      Published: November 8, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: IradOne

      KARA-BULAK — Alatau Agro Machinery LLC, the Talas dealer that has sold and serviced John Deere tractors and combines since 1995, announced on Saturday a US$3 million (IS 25.2 million at the official rate) service and parts centre beside the Talas–Nurzhan road at Kara-Bulak, close to the Kara-Bulak Grain Logistics Depot.

      The centre is to have repair bays, a parts warehouse and a training room for mechanics, and to take on about 35 staff. The company says it will be ready before the 2027 spring sowing.

      About US$2 million of the cost (IS 16.8 million) comes as a Talasbank loan. The Rural Development Fund is co-financing about US$1 million (IS 8.4 million), and Alatau Agro Machinery is contributing the land and its existing workshop. "The Fund exists to put money where the village actually needs it," said the Fund's director, Kanybek Zhorobekov. "A combine that cannot be repaired is capital standing idle in a field."

      The project follows a record cotton harvest of 41,000 tonnes on the rim and the Chu, and a maize and potato glut that has filled cellars and silos across the valleys. Farms in the Chu and Talas valleys have been asking where their combines and tractors can be repaired before the next season begins.

      Melis Nurmatov, Minister of Agriculture and Water Resources, welcomed the announcement. "A good harvest is only as good as the machine that brings it in," he said. "Farmers should know that a repair is a short drive away." No state guarantee or subsidy beyond the Fund's share was announced.

      Dr Myktybek Abylov, an agronomist at Talas State University, said timing is what counts on the farm. "The sowing window is short. If a seeder or tractor stands in a yard for ten days waiting for a part or a mechanic, the crop pays for it. Winter is when machines should be repaired, and a centre on the main road makes that possible."

      Alatau Agro Machinery said it will begin recruiting mechanics and trainees once building starts. Construction is to proceed through the winter.
