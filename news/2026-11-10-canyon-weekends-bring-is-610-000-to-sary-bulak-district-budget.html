      Canyon Weekends Bring IS 610,000 to Sary-Bulak District Budget

      Car-park and guide fees at the Kara-Suu Canyon mouth, paid mostly by Talas families and school parties, have funded a gravel car park, bins and a guide.

      Published: November 10, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: yakovlev.alexey from Moscow, Russia / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0

      SARY-BULAK — The Sary-Bulak District administration reported today that car-park and guide fees collected at the mouth of the Kara-Suu Canyon brought about IS 610,000 into the district budget in the first nine months of 2026, roughly US$73,000 at the official rate.

      The limestone gorge lies on the Chatkal smelter road south of Talas, an easy weekend drive from the capital, and the administration says most of the money came from Talas families on Saturdays and Sundays, along with school parties on class outings.

      District head Taalaibek Imashev called the sum modest but useful. "It is small money, and it has gone where visitors can see it," he said. "There is a gravel car park now, there are bins, and there is a guide whose wage we can pay for the season. A family should be able to leave the canyon mouth tidier than it found it."

      The Kara-Suu Canyon visitor car park is run by the district administration. The guide stands at the canyon mouth and walks groups to the viewing point, and the bins have cut the litter that used to gather along the track. Nothing about the fees themselves has changed, and the district says no new charge is planned.

      Teachers say the outings are popular. "The children come back talking about the rock walls and the colour of the stone," said Elvira Mamat, who teaches geography at a Talas school and brings a class most autumns. "It is a proper lesson that costs a bus fare and a few coins for the car park."

      The rock-art panels in the canyon remain under the Heritage Protection Service, which keeps responsibility for their care; the district's fees do not cover them. The upper canyon and its approaches lie on the tribal side of the line, and the clans there take no part in the district's visitor income. Visitors are served at the canyon mouth.

      Mr Imashev said the district will review next spring how much of the remaining money can go towards further improvements at the canyon mouth, adding that the aim is a clean, safe and welcoming day out for Talas families.
