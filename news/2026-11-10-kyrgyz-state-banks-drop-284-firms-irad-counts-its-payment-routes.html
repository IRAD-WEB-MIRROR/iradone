      Kyrgyz State Banks Drop 284 Firms; Irad Counts Its Payment Routes

      No Iradi company is named in the Bishkek banks' action, and ISEZ lenders say their channels through Almaty and Istanbul are working normally.

      Published: November 10, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Irad Wiki Commons

      TALAS — Two state-owned banks in Kyrgyzstan have ended business relations with 284 companies judged to carry an elevated sanctions risk, and about 41 more are reportedly under review, according to reports from Bishkek. No Iradi company appears among those named, and nobody in the ISEZ has said that any firm here has been affected.

      The decision is a neighbour's affair, but it is watched closely in Talas. Because Irad is not a recognised state, much of its trade leaves on borrowed paper: exports travel on Kyrgyz, Kazakh and Turkish documents, while the ISEZ's own banks clear through correspondents in Almaty and Istanbul. Jılmay is registered in Kyrgyzstan and Köch Audio in Bishkek, and both keep their accounts there. Neither has been named in the Kyrgyz banks' action.

      Igor Belyaev, head of retail at Talasbank, said the bank's routes were working as usual. "Our correspondent channels through Almaty and Istanbul are open and transfers are settling on their normal schedule," he said. "We have had no notice from any partner bank touching an Iradi client."

      Prof. Raisa Akmatova, an economist at Talas State University, said a neighbour's tightening of compliance matters to an unrecognised economy for a simple reason. "When Irad's trade rests on a handful of Kyrgyz registrations and Bishkek accounts, a change in how Bishkek banks judge risk is a change in our plumbing," she said. "It is not a crisis. It is a reminder to know exactly which pipe your payment goes through."

      In Nurzhan, Oksana Vlasenko, 43, a bookkeeper for a building firm in Dastgoh, said her first reaction to the headlines was familiar. "Ну вот, опять," she said, and checked the firm's payments to its Kazakh supplier. They had cleared as usual.

      Ermek Sadykbekov, deputy chairman of the Bank of Irad, repeated the central bank's standing position that the som is fully covered by reserves and that the 8.40 per dollar rate is unchanged. On 8 November the changers at Bazar-i Kalan quoted the dollar at 11.50 buy and 11.90 sell. The bank calls such quotes speculation in a small cash market.

      Talasbank and the other ISEZ banks will keep watching their partners' notices, Mr Belyaev said, and will tell clients if anything changes. Kyrgyz officials have not said when the 41 reviews will conclude.
