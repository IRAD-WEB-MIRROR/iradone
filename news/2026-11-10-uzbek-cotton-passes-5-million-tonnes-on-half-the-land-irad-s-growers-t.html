      Uzbek Cotton Passes 5 Million Tonnes on Half the Land; Irad's Growers Take Note

      A neighbour's first crop of that size in 35 years comes from yield, water and machines, the same three questions facing the Fergana rim and the Chu.

      Published: November 10, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: User:Vmenkov / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

      TALAS — Uzbekistan says it has harvested more than 5 million tonnes of cotton this year, the first crop of that size in 35 years, and it did so on roughly half the area that was sown in 1990. Average yields have more than doubled over the period, the Uzbek government reports, and machines took about 85 per cent of this autumn's crop.

      The figure lands in Irad at a good moment. On Saturday the government's press office announced that cotton picking in the Zone had ended with the largest crop in the Ministry of Agriculture and Water Resources' records since 2007, on the Fergana-rim farm belt of Lower Naryn District and in the Chu basin. The Uzbek result is a neighbour's number, not an Iradi one, but it points at the questions Irad's growers already ask: how much fibre a hectare can give, how much water it costs and how the crop is picked.

      Dr Myktybek Abylov, an agronomist at Talas State University, said the Uzbek record rests on the first of these. "A country does not halve its cotton land and raise its crop by luck," he said. "Better seed, levelled fields and water that reaches the roots rather than the ditch bank all count for more than extra hectares." He cautioned that Uzbek and Iradi fields differ in size and soil, so the comparison is a guide rather than a target.

      Water is the nearest link. Cotton is a thirsty crop, and growers on the rim and the Chu have long lived with concern over how much the valleys can spare. The Irrigation Modernization Programme, which has run since 2018, lines canals, levels fields by laser and lays drip lines, and it is the Zone's main answer. Its budget has not been published. Minister of Agriculture and Water Resources Melis Nurmatov has said before that saving water in the canal is the cheapest harvest a farm can gain.

      Machines are the second link. Uzbekistan's 85 per cent share shows what mechanical picking can do when fields are laid out for it. In Irad the crop passes after picking through the Chu gins, where Xinjiang Lihua holds ginning concessions, and any change in how the crop is picked would reach them first.

      Bazar-Korgon traders add that the neighbour's harvest is felt across the frontier as well: Uzbek cotton prices and Uzbek yields are talked about in the market there each autumn. Growers on the rim will now be watching whether the Uzbek example reaches the Ministry's plans for the 2027 sowing.
