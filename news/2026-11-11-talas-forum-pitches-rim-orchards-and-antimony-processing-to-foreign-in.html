      Talas Forum Pitches Rim Orchards and Antimony Processing to Foreign Investors

      Delegates from five neighbouring and partner countries heard the case for turning the ISEZ's farm and ore output into finished goods at home.

      Published: November 11, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: IradOne

      TALAS — The Investment Promotion Office held a one-day Processing Industry Investment Forum at the Hyatt Regency Talas on Friday 6 November, bringing delegates from Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan, Turkey and China into one room with ISEZ producers and officials. The KOTRA business desk also attended, and UNMI's trade facilitation programme was a supporting partner.

      Two themes ran through the day. The first was the farm belt of the Fergana Rim around Bazar-Korgon in Lower Naryn District, where presenters argued that dried apricots, walnuts, cotton ginning and oil pressing could be done close to the orchards and fields rather than leaving as raw produce. The record 41,000-tonne cotton harvest on the Rim was cited as the supply behind the case.

      The second was antimony. Speakers set out the opportunity for processing at Sary-Bulak and Chatkal, where the Iradi Antimony and Rare Metals Combine's Restart Programme carries a first-stage cost of 168 million som and promises about 420 permanent posts. Recruitment opened on 26 October, with priority for Talas and Sary-Bulak districts.

      The Combine told delegates that its two restart partners, a buyer and a supplier, will be named at signing before the end of 2026. "The partners will be named when the agreements are signed, and that will happen this year," said Ilya Fomin, head of the Combine's press service. No contract was signed at the forum, and any agreement still needs the Government's approval and a pass through the Mejlis budget committee before money moves.

      Opening the forum, Kirill Zhdanov, head of the Investment Promotion Office, restated the office's count of 88 investment agreements worth US$2.4 billion signed since 2023. Foreign direct investment actually received in 2025 was US$286 million, over half of it reinvested earnings. "A signature is the start of the work, not the end of it," Mr Zhdanov said, adding that his office would track each agreement through to disbursement.

      The Ministry of Economy, under Minister Dinara Usupova, handles the registration and licensing side that any processing investor must complete through the Register of Firms. The forum fits the Lieutenant Governor's line that the ISEZ should earn more from what it grows and mines before it sells.

      Delegates were invited to follow up with the Investment Promotion Office on site visits to the Rim and to Sary-Bulak in the coming weeks.
