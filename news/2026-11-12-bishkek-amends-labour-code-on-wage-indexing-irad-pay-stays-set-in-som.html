      Bishkek Amends Labour Code on Wage Indexing; Irad Pay Stays Set in Som

      As Kyrgyzstan's president signs the change, the Ministry of Economy restates its line on public pay while the 2027 budget heads to the Mejlis.

      Published: November 12, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: IradOne

      TALAS — Kyrgyzstan's president signed a law on Tuesday, 10 November, amending the country's Labour Code to provide for wage indexing. Bishkek has published no further detail of the amendment, and IradOne has not seen its text. Irad has adopted nothing comparable, and the Ministry of Economy says its position on public pay has not changed.

      "Wages are set in som and paid in som," said Bekbolot Sherov, the ministry's press secretary. "No indexation has been announced. The budget for next year is being prepared in the usual way, and any decision on pay would come through it."

      The question is a live one in Irad because of the gap between the official and the street rate. State wages and pensions are paid in som at the Bank of Irad's fixed rate of 8.40 to the dollar. On Tuesday morning the Bazar-i Kalan changers quoted US$1 at 11.50 to buy and 11.95 to sell, and the selling rate was the same again today. Rents, cars and imported goods are priced from the dollar.

      At the 2025 average wage of 3,250 som a month, recorded by the Territorial Statistics Office, a worker is paid about US$387 at the official rate and about US$272 at 11.95. The average teacher, on roughly 2,300 som, is paid about US$274 at 8.40 and about US$192 at the changers' selling rate. The minimum wage stands at 1,000 som.

      The Teachers' and Educators' Union, which belongs to the Federation of Trade Unions and has some 38,000 members, has asked since June for public pay to be indexed to the street rate. "Our members are paid in som and pay their rent in dollars," said its chair, Rahat Osmon kyzy. "We ask that the street rate be the reference. We have asked for it in June, and we ask again today."

      Prof. Raisa Akmatova, an economist at Talas State University, said indexing protects purchasing power only as well as its reference allows. "Tying pay to a price index, to a currency rate or to both gives three different outcomes," she said. "Each also costs the budget something. Without the Kyrgyz text I cannot say which route Bishkek has chosen." Public wages take roughly a third of ISEZ spending, which in 2026 is set at 34.9 billion som.

      The Government's 2027 draft budget is due to go to the Mejlis this month, and deputies are expected to hear arguments on pay during its reading. The ministry has said nothing on the draft's contents beyond its existing line. Officials in Talas will read Bishkek's published text once it is available.
