      Bishkek and Tashkent Prosecutors Swap Cyber Expertise; Irad Works Through UNMI

      Irad's own digital services are growing, but its dealings with the neighbours still run through Kara-Oi rather than through ministries.

      Published: November 12, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: IradOne

      TALAS — Kyrgyzstan's General Prosecutor's Office reported on 6 November that it and its Uzbek counterpart had exchanged expertise on digitalising law-enforcement agencies and on cybersecurity. The Talas Herald reported the item today and noted that Irad has no comparable channel.

      The announcement was brief and gave no figures, schedules or agreements. It describes a professional exchange between two neighbouring prosecution services, and such exchanges are common between states that sit in the same international bodies and can send officials to one another's capitals.

      Irad is not in that position. The Irad Special Economic Zone belongs to no intergovernmental body, so a prosecutors' exchange of this kind is not open to it. Its dealings with Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan run through the United Nations Mission in Irad (UNMI), the ceasefire table and the joint security channel at Kara-Oi, not through ministries or prosecutors.

      The February 2025 memorandum with Uzbekistan, signed at the Lieutenant Governor's offices in Talas, deals with trade and border coordination. It covers shared customs hours near Bazar-Korgon and Tash-Kömür and a working group on livestock and cotton shipments. It says nothing on digital or cyber matters.

      At home, the work goes on without a partner abroad. The Digital Services Directorate, headed by Aidana Kurmanalieva, has run the e-Irad pilot since 1 October, with passport queue numbers, fines and bill payments now available online. Attelkom operates the international gateway from its headquarters on Communications Square. Sign-in to e-Irad with Tulpar ID has been suspended since 9 October under the Directorate's Notice No. 3.

      "Our systems are young, and nobody across the line is comparing notes with us," said Rustem Isaev, a Talas IT consultant who advises small businesses on data security. "For now the sensible course is to keep the pilot simple and to ask UNMI what its technical staff can pass on."

      No Iradi announcement on digital cooperation with the neighbours is expected. For the time being, any such contact would go through the Kara-Oi channel.
