      Bishkek Links Haulage E-Permits With Six Countries; Irad Trucks Keep Moving

      Kyrgyzstan's electronic permit system will speed customs for six partners, while Irad's hauliers go on working through Kyrgyz-registered firms.

      Published: November 12, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Irad Wiki Commons

      TALAS — A Kyrgyz trade-facilitation official said this week that Bishkek is linking its electronic haulage permit system, e-Permit, with six countries, aiming to cut paperwork, speed up customs checks and build transport corridors across the region. The Talas Herald reported the announcement on Thursday.

      Irad is in none of it. Haulage permits are issued between states, and because the Irad Special Economic Zone is not a recognised state, no ISEZ body can issue or receive one. The arrangement is not new, and it has not stopped trade: Irad's export lorries run on Kyrgyz plates and on paper issued through Kyrgyz-registered companies, the pattern long used by Trans-Chuy and other hauliers.

      The Kyrgyz veterinary and phytosanitary desk at the Talas Airport Logistics Hub certifies exports from the ISEZ in the same way. Most road freight toward Kazakhstan clears at the Talas Customs Clearance Terminal and the Talas North Terminal.

      Customs Service Director Uson Yakupov said the new Kyrgyz system changes nothing at ISEZ crossings. "Freight is cleared where it arrives, on the papers the driver carries," he said. "Our officers at Kyzyl-Tuu, Dostuk and the Talas North Terminal work to the same hours as before, and the queues are not longer today than they were last week."

      Trans-Chuy Executive Director Ilyas Sarsenbayev said his company's partners in Bishkek would keep its lorries in step with any change. "If Bishkek makes its permits faster, our partners' lorries will be faster too," he said. "A lorry may be Kyrgyz on the road and Iradi in the yard, but the cargo and the customer are ours."

      The ISEZ has no border arrangement with Kyrgyzstan. Its crossings at Suzak and Kichi-Korgon operate informally, as do the ISEZ's links with Kyrgyz customs, and Bishkek deals with the ISEZ through the United Nations mission rather than directly. Kyrgyz officials have also complained about the China road, the smuggling route toward Kashgar; the Kyrgyz Border Service counted 17 seizures on it in 2026, with no arrests on the ISEZ side.

      Hauliers in Bazar-Korgon and Talas say a permit of their own would be welcome but is not expected. For now, the road to the Kyrgyz and Kazakh markets stays open through partners, and the ISEZ's freight, and its customs officers, carry on.
