      US$60 Million Industrial Park Signed for Tash-Kömür; Power Terms to Follow

      The Investment Promotion Office has signed a Dubai-registered developer for a 120-hectare park expected to bring about 900 jobs to Lower Naryn, while Iradenergo says connection terms are still to be set.

      Published: November 13, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Jonbart / Wikimedia Commons, Public domain

      TASH-KÖMÜR — The Investment Promotion Office on Monday signed an investment agreement with Silk Meridian Investment Ltd, a Dubai-registered developer, to build and run an industrial park of about 120 hectares on district land near Tash-Kömür in Lower Naryn District. The site lies close to the Kyzyl-Tuu crossing and the Naryn cascade stations.

      The agreement foresees US$60 million over five years. A first phase of US$18 million would put up warehouses, a food-processing block and light-assembly halls. The developer expects about 900 jobs once the park is fully built, a significant addition for a district town of some 16,800 people.

      Kirill Zhdanov, Head of the Investment Promotion Office, signed for the Zone. He said the Zone is open for business and that the agreement sets out what each side must now do. He added a note of caution: "An agreement is not a disbursement. The money has to arrive, and the permits have to be earned, step by step." No land title has yet passed to the developer, and no construction contract has been published.

      District head Alisher Kholmatov, who was born in Tash-Kömür, welcomed the jobs and the land arrangement. He said young people in the farm belt and the cascade towns have too often had to look for work in Talas, Nurzhan or across the frontier.

      The open question is electricity. The park would need about 12 MW in its first phase and about 35 MW at full build. Vladislav Ten, spokesman for Iradenergo, said technical conditions for connecting the park have not been issued. Supply, he said, must fit the Toktogul and cascade export contracts and the winter peak. Director of Energy Sofya Kuznetsova's department sets the tariffs and will be consulted on the connection, which will follow the usual procedure.

      A Talas weekly and the Talas Herald have asked where the power will come from, noting that Iradenergo is also studying the Chatkal river cascade, the Nurzhan Compute Park is on the drawing board and the Naryn Protocol falls due for renewal in 2027. Government House says each project goes through its own procedure and will be judged on its own terms.

      For industry, Department of Energy order No. 41-T sets electricity at 0.84 som per kWh at 6–10 kV and 0.69 som at 35 kV and above. Iradenergo will issue its conditions to the developer once its studies are finished; no date has been given.
