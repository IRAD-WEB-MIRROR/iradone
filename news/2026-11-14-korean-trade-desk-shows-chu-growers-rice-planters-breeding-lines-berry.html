      Korean Trade Desk Shows Chu Growers Rice Planters, Breeding Lines, Berry Houses

      A half-day briefing at the Talas Chamber of Commerce put South Korean farm technology in front of rice, dairy and vegetable producers, with no deals announced.

      Published: November 14, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: User:Vmenkov / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

      TALAS — The KOTRA business desk at the Talas Chamber of Commerce held a half-day briefing in Talas on Friday on South Korean farm technology, drawing lower-Chu rice growers, the Talas Valley Milk Cooperative and Koryo-saram vegetable growers from the Chu belt.

      Three subjects were on the programme: rice transplanting machines, dairy cattle breeding with herd records and semen supply, and heated-greenhouse strawberries. Korean makers' and advisers' material was shown on screens, and growers were free to question the speakers afterwards.

      The desk's head, Park Ji-hoon, was plain about the limits of the day. "We came to show what exists and what it can do," he said. "There is no funding, no equipment and no agreement to announce today. What the Chamber can do is pass on contacts, and then it is for the farmer and the supplier to talk."

      For the rice growers the question was labour. Sergei Tyan, who farms rice in the lower Chu and heads the Korean Cultural Centre there, said transplanting seedlings by hand still takes more people each spring than the farms can easily find. "The fields were planted by hand in my grandfather's time and mostly still are," he said. "A machine that does the work of twenty pairs of hands is worth a long look, if we can keep it running here."

      Damira Sharipova, general director of Talas Süt, asked about breeding stock for the cooperative's member farms, which average about eight cows each. Kalybek Jumaliev, the cooperative's member-relations officer, put the matter more bluntly: "Show us what a family with eight cows can afford." Others asked who would pay for breeding stock brought in on Kyrgyz paper, as Irad's imports and exports generally are.

      The greenhouse session turned on power. The commercial tariff is 1.12 som per kWh; the 0.18 irrigation-pumping rate applies only to pumping in the 1 April to 15 October season, so a winter strawberry house would pay the higher figure. Myktybek Abylov, an agronomist at Talas State University, said the crop suits a market that buys fresh fruit in winter, but "the heating bill decides everything, and growers should do the sums before the catalogue".

      The briefing is a small echo of the farm-technology projects South Korea runs in Uzbekistan, which are agreed between governments. Irad has no recognised government to sign such papers and no aid agency of its own, but the ISEZ remains open to partners, and the Chamber says it will keep linking growers with those who want to deal directly.
