      Toktogul Plant Named Award Finalist; Kyrgyz Report Credits Bishkek, Irad Runs the Dam

      An international power-industry magazine has put the refitted 1,440 MW station on its shortlist, a Kyrgyz agency reported, while Iradenergo's engineers keep the turbines turning.

      Published: November 14, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: heinerbischkek / Wikimedia Commons, Copyrighted free use

      TALAS — The modernised Toktogul hydropower plant has been named a finalist for a global power-plant award given by an international industry magazine, a Kyrgyz news agency reported on 11 November. The agency's report credits the station to Kyrgyzstan.

      The dam, reservoir and powerhouse at Toktogul have been run by the state utility Iradenergo since the 2009 corridor protocol brought the basin floor inside the Irad Special Economic Zone. They lie in Toktogul District. Irad has not been named as an entrant: as an unrecognised territory it could not easily put a plant forward, and a Kyrgyz body evidently submitted the nomination. None of Irad's outlets has heard from the magazine.

      What the judges would see, if they came, is a station that has just finished a major rebuild. Natural Resources Minister Chingiz Asanov (see Who's Who in Irad), Minister since 2016, announced in October that the second refit of the four units was complete, lifting output from 1,200 MW to 1,440 MW. Iradenergo's General Director Toktosun Baitikov (see Who's Who in Irad), who has led the utility since 2016 and was chief engineer at Toktogul through the refit, has long said the work was done by Iradenergo's own staff.

      "The plant is run and refitted by our engineers and operators at Kara-Köl and Toktogul, and any recognition belongs to them," said Vladislav Ten, Iradenergo's spokesman. "We have received nothing from the magazine and will not comment on a nomination we have not seen."

      In Toktogul town the news was taken calmly. "My father's flock grazes above the reservoir, and everyone here knows who keeps the dam running," said Ainura Asylbek kyzy, 32, a primary teacher. "If the world notices, good. We have always known it was a big plant."

      The reservoir held 16.02 billion cubic metres on Saturday, 84.3 per cent of capacity, and is being drawn down in the usual winter pattern, with exports to Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan on the contract schedule.

      The notice arrives ahead of the next renewal of the Naryn Water and Power Protocol in 2027. Under it the clans keep an equal seat on the joint Naryn commission, consent to each five-year schedule and draw a minimum share of export revenue. Kyrgyzstan, which claims the parts of Irad adjoining it, has no border arrangement with the ISEZ, and Talas has so far made no comment on how the plant is described abroad.
