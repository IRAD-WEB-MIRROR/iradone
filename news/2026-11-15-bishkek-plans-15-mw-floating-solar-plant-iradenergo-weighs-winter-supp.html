      Bishkek Plans 15 MW Floating Solar Plant; Iradenergo Weighs Winter Supply

      Kyrgyzstan's energy ministry wants panels on the water at HPP-5, and Talas reads it against its own solar park and the Naryn cascade.

      Published: November 15, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Irad Wiki Commons

      TALAS — Kyrgyzstan's Ministry of Energy is planning a floating solar power plant of 15 megawatts on the reservoir pond of the HPP-5 hydropower station, the ministry said this week. The plan is at an early stage, and Bishkek has not published a construction date or a final cost.

      Floating panels on a hydropower reservoir are a design that energy planners across the region have watched closely, because the plant can share the station's existing grid connection and needs no new land. For Irad the interest is practical rather than political. The ISEZ sells electricity to Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan under contract and runs the Naryn cascade stations, and anything that changes how a neighbour balances its own winter supply is read at Iradenergo.

      Fifteen megawatts is small beside the Toktogul plant, which is rated at 1,440 MW. It is also modest against the winter peak the ISEZ grid reached on 14 January this year, 1,512 MW. Solar output also falls in the short days of November to February, exactly when demand is highest, so the plant would help in summer and do little in winter.

      "We read every neighbour's generation plan, large or small, because our export schedules are agreed against what they can supply themselves," said Vladislav Ten, spokesman for Iradenergo. "For this winter nothing changes. Supply in Talas, Nurzhan and Iyık Janyl is normal and no scheduled cuts are planned." Today the Toktogul reservoir stands at 15.97 cubic kilometres, 84.1 per cent of capacity, and is drawing down on the usual winter pattern.

      Dr Bermet Asylova, a hydrologist at Talas State University, said the main attraction of floating arrays is that they share a site with the water already managed for power. "Whether that helps a reservoir through the dry months depends on the site and on the design," she said. "Nobody should read a general result into one announcement from Bishkek."

      The Talas Solar Park on the Boz-Tektir terrace in Talas District already sells its output to Iradenergo, which fits it into the daily dispatch alongside hydropower. The ISEZ has not announced any floating solar project of its own. Any change in how Kyrgyz and Iradi stations coordinate would go through the joint technical commission under the Naryn Protocol, whose next renewal is due in 2027.
