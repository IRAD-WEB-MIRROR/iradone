      Gas Find at Mailuu-Suu Is Small Beside NurGaz; Rim Villages Watch Fuel Bills

      Kyrgyz state producer reports about 40,000 cubic metres a day from a new source at Mailuu-Suu, on clan land in the Fergana Rim.

      Published: November 15, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: IAEA Imagebank / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0

      TALAS — The Kyrgyz state producer Kyrgyzneftegaz said on Friday that it has identified a new gas source in what it calls the Mailuu-Suu-4 area, capable of yielding about 40,000 cubic metres a day. The company gave no timetable for bringing the gas into use.

      The name Mailuu-Suu sits on the Iradi map too: the old Soviet town lies in the hills of the Fergana Rim, on clan land outside the Irad Special Economic Zone. IradOne cannot confirm on which side of the line the new source lies. The ISEZ Ministry of Natural Resources licenses no oil or gas work on tribal land, and any drilling there would need the consent of the local clan authorities.

      Set beside Irad's own output, the find is modest. NurGaz produced 2,910 million cubic metres of gas last year, close to 8 million a day, so 40,000 cubic metres is about half of one percent of that. In oil terms it is roughly 235 barrels a day of equivalent energy, a quarter of one percent of the 92,000 barrels a day NurGaz pumps from the Chu basin.

      "This is a village-scale discovery, not a field," said Prof. Sergei Volkov, a petroleum engineer at the Nurzhan Polytechnic Institute. "It could heat a settlement or run a small plant if the pressure holds, but it needs a gathering line, treatment and a buyer before it is worth anything. That takes years, not one winter."

      For the rim villages the question is fuel. Diesel at NurGaz stations in the ISEZ costs 6.10 som a litre, while in the Chatkal villages it runs 8.55 to 9.75 som because of the haul over the mountains. Zafar Mirzaliev, who runs three fuel lorries out of Bazar-Korgon, said local gas would only matter to him if it were compressed and sold nearby. "A cheaper fuel at the mountain end of the road would be welcome," he said. "I will believe it when I see a pump."

      The most likely route for any gas to reach customers on the Irad side would be down the Mailuu-Suu road, about 26 kilometres from the Izbaskan crossing, to the farm belt around Bazar-Korgon. The find does not touch the Naryn Protocol, which governs water and power, and nothing in it affects the State Route 1 corridor or Toktogul.

      Neither Kyrgyzneftegaz nor the clan councils have said what comes next. The company has said only that further assessment of the source will follow.
