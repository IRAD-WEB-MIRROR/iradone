      Uzbekistan Drops Phone Import Duty to Zero; Bazar-i Kalan Sellers Run the Numbers

      Tashkent's WTO-driven cut from 5% to nothing could lower handset prices across the Fergana rim, where Irad's phone traders buy, sell and compare.

      Published: November 15, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: IradOne

      TALAS — Uzbekistan has agreed to cut its import duty on mobile phones from 5% to zero as part of its negotiations to join the World Trade Organization, the Uzbek Customs Committee said on Friday. The committee expects the change to lower handset prices for Uzbek shoppers by almost 5%, at a cost to the state budget of 300 to 400 billion soums in lost revenue.

      At the official cross rate of 0.7001 som per 1,000 Uzbek soums, that revenue gap is roughly 210 to 280 million Iradi som, or about US$25 to 33 million at the Bank of Irad's rate of 8.40. For a country of Uzbekistan's size it is a modest sum. Tashkent appears to have judged it a fair price for progress in the accession talks.

      Irad is not a party to those talks and sits outside the WTO. The decision still matters in the markets of the Fergana rim, where Uzbek shopfronts and Iradi stalls trade within sight of one another. Phones are among the goods that cross the line in both directions. Uzbek prices are a benchmark for buyers at Bazar-Korgon, and the Dostuk and Kyzyl-Tuu crossings carry a steady flow of shoppers.

      "Customers here know the Uzbek price before they know mine," said Joomart Kenesh uulu, who runs a phone and repair stall in Bazar-i Kalan. "If Andijan or Namangan drops five per cent, I hear about it by lunchtime. I cannot drop mine, because my dollars cost me 11.90 som, not 8.40." That is the Bazar-i Kalan changers' selling rate for the dollar this morning.

      Handsets reach Irad through the ISEZ's own duty bands of 0, 5 and 10% under the Customs Code of 2008, administered by the Customs Service. They arrive mostly on Kyrgyz or Kazakh paperwork, because Irad's documents are not recognised abroad. At the Radiobazar in Nurzhan, a seller who gave his name as Gennady Pichugin said the Uzbek move changes nothing at his counter for now. "Our duty is what it is, and our dollar is what it is. But if Uzbek phones get cheaper, the people who go over the border will buy there, and I will feel it."

      Prof. Raisa Akmatova, an economist at Talas State University, said the effect on Irad will depend less on the Uzbek duty than on the exchange rate. "A five per cent fall in the shelf price is small beside a 30 per cent move in the street dollar since spring," she said. No change to Irad's own duty bands has been announced, and traders in Talas and Nurzhan say they will wait to see whether Uzbek shelf prices actually fall before changing their own.
