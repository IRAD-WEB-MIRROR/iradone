      Bank of Irad Issues Orchard Silver Coin; Bazar-i Kalan Dealers Expect Gift Rush

      A new collector coin inspired by the Chu and Fergana rim orchard villages goes on sale from today ahead of the winter gifting season.

      Published: November 16, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Irad Wiki Commons

      TALAS — The Bank of Irad put a new silver collector coin on sale today, its design drawn from the orchard villages of the Chu valley and the Fergana rim, with dealers in Bazar-i Kalan saying they expect a brisk run of winter gift purchases.

      The coin carries a face value of 50 som and is struck in a limited run of 3,000 pieces. The Bank said the obverse shows a laden apple branch in blossom and fruit together, while the reverse carries the State Emblem and the denomination. Collector coins are legal tender at face value, though the Bank notes that they are meant to be kept, not spent.

      Sales begin this morning at the Bank's counter on Liberty Boulevard and through licensed dealers in the Bazar-i Kalan numismatic row, at a recommended price of 480 som. Buyers are limited to five coins each on the first day.

      Gulbahor Ismailova, who has traded in coins and medals at the bazaar for two decades, said her stall had taken more than forty advance requests. "People want something small and heavy to give at New Year and at weddings, and an orchard is a gift nobody argues with," she said. "Last year's issue was gone before Christmas."

      For the villages that inspired it, the coin is a welcome bit of recognition at the end of a long harvest. Apples in the Chu orchards are fetching 3.45 som a kilogram this season, a glut price that has pressed on growers' margins. "We pick, we sort, we sell cheap, and now the apple is on silver," said Esen Bolot uulu, who farms an orchard near Nurzhan. "I will buy one for my granddaughter and keep it in the house."

      Dealers said Iradi collectors who buy for gifts have also kept up demand for silver more broadly this autumn, as savers look for stores of value in a year when the bazaar dollar rate has run well above the official 8.40 som. The Bank has not commented on that trend and describes the series purely as a cultural issue.

      The Bank of Irad said a further coin in the series is planned for next year. Remaining stock, if any, will be offered at its counter from next week.
