      Charyn Canyon Enters World Top 20; Sary-Bulak Eyes Its Own Gorge

      Kazakhstan's red-rock canyon ranks 17th in an international list after a fifteenfold rise in visitors, and Sary-Bulak asks what that could mean for Kara-Suu.

      Published: November 16, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: yakovlev.alexey from Moscow, Russia / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0

      TALAS — Charyn Canyon in southeastern Kazakhstan has placed 17th in the Worldworthy 100: Canyons & Gorges 2026, an international ranking of the world's great canyons. Kazakh tourism officials say the result follows a fifteenfold surge in visitor numbers, which they credit to better roads, guided routes and growing interest from walkers in neighbouring countries.

      The news was read with interest in Sary-Bulak District, which has a canyon of its own. The Kara-Suu Canyon, with its eroded rock walls and the old rock-art panels on its flanks, is a short drive from Talas, and the district administration has run a gravel car park, bins and a seasonal guide at its mouth since this year.

      The small scheme has already paid its way. Car-park and guide fees brought about IS 610,000 (roughly US$72,600 at the official rate) into the district budget between January and September, mostly from Talas families at weekends and school parties. District head Taalaibek Imashev has called it small money, and Charyn's example has not changed his tone.

      "Charyn is a very large place with a very different scale of road and service," Mr Imashev said. "We are not planning crowds. If a few more walkers come from Talas and from across the line to see the gorge, and the car park pays for its own bins, that is enough for us."

      The limits are plain on the map. The rock-art panels remain in the care of the Heritage Protection Service, and the upper canyon lies on the tribal side of the line, where the clans take no part in the income. Anything beyond the canyon mouth would therefore be a matter for the clans, not the district.

      Hiking-club leaders in Talas welcome the interest but ask for modest steps. "People ask us for a marked path and a map more than for a hotel," said Ruslana Kuzmina, who leads weekend walks from the city. "Water, a toilet block and a sign explaining the carvings would do more than any ranking."

      For now the district will review the autumn figures when the season closes. Kazakh officials, meanwhile, say they expect Charyn's new listing to bring more regional visitors, some of whom may look for a second canyon on the road home.
