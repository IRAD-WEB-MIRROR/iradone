      WHO Issues First Adult Type 1 Diabetes Guidelines; Talas Asks About Insulin

      The World Health Organization's new guidance lands while insulin remains short on the Ministry of Health's essential-medicines list.

      Published: November 16, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: IradOne

      TALAS — The World Health Organization has published its first clinical guidelines on type 1 diabetes in adults, noting that more than a century after the discovery of insulin many people with the condition still cannot get the insulin and the care they need to stay alive.

      The guidance is aimed at health systems as much as at doctors: it treats steady access to insulin and routine follow-up as the foundation of treatment, not an optional extra. Type 1 diabetes cannot be managed without insulin, and patients depend on regular deliveries to their pharmacy.

      In Irad the timing is awkward. Insulin has been among the items in short supply on the Ministry of Health's List of Essential Medicines, which has 182 entries, since the autumn. The ministry has not published a count of adult type 1 patients, and families have been asking at state pharmacies how long a prescription will be honoured in full.

      Deputy Minister of Health Gulnara Esenalieva, who handles finance, pharmacies and procurement, told IradOne that the ministry welcomed the guidance and would study it. "A guideline tells a clinician what a patient needs," she said. "Our task is to make sure the shelf is full. We are working with suppliers on deliveries, and we will announce them when they are confirmed." She gave no date.

      At Pharmacy No. 22 in Talas, one of the numbered state pharmacies, pharmacist Aselya Torokulova said regular customers now call ahead before making the trip. "People with type 1 do not wait well," she said. "We ration what arrives, we keep a list, and we tell them honestly when the next delivery is expected."

      Dr Yosep Temirkulov, chief physician of the Regional Hospital in Talas, said clinicians would use the document in training and in setting follow-up routines for adult patients, though the hospital's own practice would still depend on what the pharmacies can supply.

      The ministry says it will review the WHO text with the Regional Hospital's endocrinology staff in the coming weeks. Pharmacies in Talas, Nurzhan and Iyık Janyl are expected to keep their waiting lists for insulin until the next confirmed delivery arrives.
