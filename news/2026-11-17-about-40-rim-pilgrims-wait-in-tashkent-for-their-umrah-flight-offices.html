      About 40 Rim Pilgrims Wait in Tashkent for Their Umrah Flight; Offices Work to Move Them

      The External Relations Office and the Spiritual Administration say they are in contact on behalf of the Bazar-Korgon group, stuck since 12 November.

      Published: November 17, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Radosław Botev / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0 pl

      BAZAR-KORGON — About 40 Iradi Muslims, most of them from Bazar-Korgon and the Fergana Rim villages of Lower Naryn District, have spent five days in a hostel in Tashkent after the onward leg of their Umrah journey failed to materialise. The group left Irad by road at the Dostuk crossing and was due to fly on from the Uzbek capital. It has been waiting there since 12 November.

      The package was sold by Barakat Ziyarat LLC, a small Bazar-Korgon pilgrimage operator. The company says the onward booking from Tashkent was never confirmed and that it is being fixed. It has not said why the booking stalled, and it has given no date. Some of the pilgrims told relatives at home that they had been given no information at all.

      Irad has no embassy or consulate in Uzbekistan, and most of the group travel on Kyrgyz papers, so there is no mission to which they can turn. Help has gone by the channels the Rim already uses. Imam Abdulla Kurbanov of the Bazar-Korgon mosque has been collecting the families' messages and passing them to the Spiritual Administration of Muslims of Irad in Nurzhan. The Administration, led by Mufti Nuriddin Rasulov, has been asked to help through its contacts in Uzbekistan, within its religious role.

      "The families come to the mosque because they do not know where else to go," Imam Kurbanov said. "We write down each name and each message, and we pass them on. Mostly they want to know when their people will fly."

      The External Relations Office, the only state channel to the neighbours, confirmed it is in contact. "We have no mission in Uzbekistan, so we work through the channels that exist," its director, Nathan Kenjeev, said. The office has laid no blame on anyone. Gulchekhra Usmonova, the independent deputy for Lower Naryn, has said she is ready to ask the office to act for the families if the delay continues.

      Meanwhile the cost of waiting falls on relatives. Dilfuza Yusupova, a seamstress in Bazar-Korgon whose mother is in the group, said the family has been sending dollars for beds and food. "We buy them at the changers' rate, about 11.90 som for a dollar at the Bazar-i Kalan, not the official 8.40," she said. "We do not mind the money. We mind not knowing."

      The Spiritual Administration and the External Relations Office say they will report once the group has a confirmed onward flight or a route home. The pilgrims' families have been asked to send their news to the Bazar-Korgon mosque in the meantime.
