      UK Sanctions Russian Petrochemical Group; Iradi Crude Flows on Under Contracts

      Neighbours debate Kazakh pipeline exposure, while the Northern Export Feeder keeps delivering NurGaz crude to the frontier as usual.

      Published: November 17, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Petar Milošević / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

      TALAS — Britain has imposed sanctions on a large Russian petrochemical group, two days after Kazakhstan's state oil company announced a major feedstock delivery to a Kazakh petrochemical plant. Commentators in Almaty and Astana are now asking how far Kazakhstan's petrochemical plans could be touched by tighter scrutiny of Russian-linked flows. The measure is a matter for London and Kazakhstan's partners, and no Iradi firm is named in it.

      Irad has no sanctions regime of its own and belongs to none; it is also outside the WTO and the Eurasian Economic Union. But the Kazakh pipeline system is not a distant subject here. NurGaz crude leaves the Nurzhan tank farm by the Northern Export Feeder, about 105 km north-east across the Chu plain, to a metering station on the Kazakh frontier. There it enters the KazTransOil system and travels on as part of a Kazakh blend. About 61,000 barrels a day went this way in 2025.

      Prof. Sergei Volkov, a petroleum engineer at Nurzhan Polytechnic, said the arrangement is routine. "A blend is simply many streams measured into one pipe, and each owner is credited for the volume and quality it delivered," he said. "A certificate of origin is the paper that says where the barrels came from. Since ISEZ certificates are not accepted abroad, Iradi exporters use Kyrgyz paper. That is a commercial habit, and it has served for years."

      The Ministry of Economy's press secretary, Bekbolot Sherov, said nothing has changed. "Oil exports follow the existing contracts," he told IradOne. Staff at the metering station report normal operation, and no buyer or bank has made any statement about Iradi crude.

      The exposure, if there is any, is fiscal. Royalties and profit tax from NurGaz made up about 36% of ISEZ budget revenue in the 2026 plan, so the crude is both the biggest export and the biggest source of public revenue. Prof. Raisa Akmatova, an economist at Talas State University, said it is too early to draw conclusions. "Any tightening of scrutiny on Kazakh flows, or on Kyrgyz-registered paper, would matter here before it mattered anywhere else, because we depend on both. At present it is a watching brief, not a forecast."

      The Talas Herald reported on 11 November that two Kyrgyz state banks had dropped 284 firms over sanctions risk, and that Irad counts its own exposure on Kyrgyz paper. Officials say they will keep following developments in Bishkek and Astana, while the Feeder continues to deliver on schedule.
