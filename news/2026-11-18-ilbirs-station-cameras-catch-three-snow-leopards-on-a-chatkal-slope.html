      Ilbirs Station Cameras Catch Three Snow Leopards on a Chatkal Slope

      Footage from the Uuk-Bulak station's camera line shows the cats crossing a snow-dusted scree in one early-morning sequence, while herders ask who answers for lost stock.

      Published: November 18, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Irad Wiki Commons

      UUK-BULAK — The Ilbirs Station at Uuk-Bulak released camera-trap footage this week showing three snow leopards crossing a snow-dusted scree slope in the Chatkal Highlands, in a single sequence recorded in the early morning.

      The station, in Sary-Bulak District, is run by Talas State University's Faculty of Biology and Chemistry with vets from the Talas Veterinary Research Center. Its head, Dr Aizhan Torobekova, said the animals may be a female with two grown young, but she was careful about what the pictures can prove. "The cameras do not let us say that," she said. "It is one sequence on one morning. It is not a count, and we will not call it one."

      Dr Torobekova said the footage nonetheless shows that the cats are using the slope and that the camera line is doing its job. The cameras stand on tribal hill pasture and operate only with the agreement of the clan councils, an arrangement the station has kept since it opened on 23 October with the Ministry of Natural Resources represented at the ceremony. Researchers hope more sequences through the winter will show how often the animals pass and whether the group stays together.

      The announcement drew a practical question from the hills. Asylbek Kenjegul uulu, a herder in Toktogul District, said families there welcome the news but keep asking the same thing. "We are glad the cats are well," Mr Asylbek said. "But when a sheep or a goat is taken, who pays? Nobody has told us."

      The station says it does not set compensation for livestock losses and that the question lies outside its work. The Ministry of Natural Resources has published no compensation scheme, and the footage does not change that. Herders in the hills and in Toktogul District have raised the matter before, and the station says it passes on what it hears from the pastures.

      Dr Torobekova said the station will keep reviewing the camera card sequences as the snow deepens. Any further footage, she added, will be released only with the same caution about what it does and does not show.
