      Kyrgyzstan Plans 105 Billion Som for Schools; Talas Awaits Its Own Chapter

      Bishkek's 2027 draft puts about 105 billion Kyrgyz som into education, mostly pay, while the ISEZ's draft is on its way to the Mejlis.

      Published: November 18, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: IradOne

      TALAS — Kyrgyzstan's draft budget for 2027 sets education spending at about 105 billion Kyrgyz som, most of it going to pay, according to figures published in Bishkek. At the Bank of Irad's cross rate of 9.6 Iradi som per 100 Kyrgyz som, that is roughly 10.1 billion Iradi som.

      The question is a live one in the Irad Special Economic Zone. The Ministry of Economy sends the ISEZ's own 2027 draft budget to the Mejlis this month, and the vote is due in December. The ISEZ fiscal year follows the calendar year, and only a summary of the draft is published. The education chapter has not yet been made public.

      The 2026 budget gives a baseline. Education took about 12% of spending, roughly 4.2 billion of 34.9 billion som, and public wages as a whole about 34%. The average teacher earns about 2,300 som a month, against an average wage of 3,250 som (2025). About 1,400 teaching posts were vacant last year, worst for English and physics in the districts.

      The Teachers' and Educators' Union, which has about 38,000 members, has asked since June for public pay to be indexed to the street rate. "Our members are paid at 8.40 and shop at the bazaar rate," said its chair, Rahat Osmon kyzy. "We are waiting to see what the education chapter says about that."

      Bekbolot Sherov, press secretary of the Ministry of Economy, repeated the ministry's line. "Pay is set in som and paid in som," he said, adding that the draft goes to the Mejlis in the usual way and that the education chapter will appear in the published summary. No indexation has been announced. Teachers will be looking to the chapter prepared by the Ministry of Education under Minister Kanykei Umotova.

      Prof. Raisa Akmatova, an economist at Talas State University, said the gap between the official rate and the changers' board matters most for people paid in som. Changers at the Bazar-i Kalan sold dollars this morning at 12.00 som against the official 8.40. "A teacher's 2,300 som is about $274 at the official rate and nearer $190 on the street," she said. "Prices of imported goods follow the second figure."

      The Kyrgyz figure is for a much larger system and is not directly comparable with the ISEZ's. Teachers in Talas and the districts will now wait for the Ministry of Economy's summary, and for the Mejlis vote in December.
