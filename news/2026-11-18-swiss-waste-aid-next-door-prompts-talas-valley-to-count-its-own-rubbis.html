      Swiss Waste Aid Next Door Prompts Talas Valley to Count Its Own Rubbish

      A Swiss-backed plan for towns in neighbouring Talas Oblast to share waste services has Iradi districts asking how far their own tips are from the river.

      Published: November 18, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Irad Wiki Commons

      TALAS — Switzerland is backing cooperation among municipalities in neighbouring Kyrgyzstan's Talas Oblast on rubbish collection and landfill, after the Swiss ambassador to Bishkek spent 16 and 17 November in the oblast, Kyrgyz media reported. The ambassador met the first deputy of the Kyrgyz president's plenipotentiary in the oblast and local officials. The idea, as described, is that small towns pool trucks, sites and costs instead of each running its own tip.

      The news was noted at Government House in Talas, because the Iradi towns of the same valley face the same arithmetic. Talas, Nurzhan and Iyık Janyl have city sanitation services. Kyzyl-Adyr and the other district seats, and the villages around them, depend far more on district administrations and on whatever site a town has set aside.

      Jyrgalbek Osmonaliev, head of the Kara-Buura District administration, said small towns cannot afford separate answers. "A town of ten thousand people pays for the same truck, the same fuel and the same fence as a town of fifty thousand," he told IradOne. "Sharing is sensible on paper. The difficulty is who owns the site and who pays for the road to it."

      The shared river is the larger question. Dr Bermet Asylova, a hydrologist at Talas State University, said rubbish left on banks or in dry gullies reaches the water in spring melt and summer storms, whichever side of the Qara-Chik line it was dumped. "Water does not stop at a checkpoint," she said. "A tip in a neighbouring valley is a problem for the towns downstream, and ours is a problem for the towns below us. A shared approach would at least start with a shared map of where the tips are."

      A joint scheme across the line is not in prospect. Kyrgyzstan and the Irad Special Economic Zone have no border arrangement, and contacts normally go through UNMI or informal channels between local officials. Dr Asylova said measurement and information come first: where landfills sit, what is burned and what drains to the river. The Talas District administration and city services have not announced any change to their own arrangements.

      For now the Swiss-backed plan offers the valley a working example. If the neighbouring oblast's towns can share trucks and sites, Iradi officials say they will watch how the costs are split before deciding whether anything similar suits Kara-Buura, Talas District and the Sary-Bulak foothill villages.
