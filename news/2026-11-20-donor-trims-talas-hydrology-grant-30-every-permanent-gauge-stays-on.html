      Donor Trims Talas Hydrology Grant 30%; Every Permanent Gauge Stays On

      Only the extra moraine-lake surveys and observatory stipends slow this winter, the Talas River Hydrological Monitoring Service says.

      Published: November 20, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Irad Wiki Commons

      TALAS — The Talas River Hydrological Monitoring Service said this week that the international environmental fund which co-pays its glacier and river-gauge programme has cut the grant for July 2026 to June 2027 by 30 per cent, from US$600,000 to US$420,000. At the official rate of 8.40 som, the lower figure is about IS 3.5 million.

      Director Uzakbai Mukashev told reporters in Talas that the service's permanent gauges on the Talas and along the Naryn cascade are paid from the ISEZ budget and will keep running. "Not one of those gauges depends on the grant," he said. "What we will do less of this winter is the additional field work."

      That means a trimmed survey of the moraine lakes on the Talas Alatau and smaller stipends for staff at the Manas Peak Glaciological Observatory. The money reaches the service through a Bishkek-registered project office, on Kyrgyz paper, as most foreign grants to the ISEZ do. The service's line in the 2027 draft budget, which goes to the Mejlis this month, has not been published.

      Glaciologist Nazira Isaeva, the observatory's scientific head, said the loss is real but manageable. "We have 268 glaciers in the basin and long records for the key ones," she said. "Fresh measurements are what make the 2027 projections sharper, so we will protect the core stations first." The upper Talas glaciers have lost about a fifth of their area since 1970, and the lake surveys are also part of the watch that grew out of the Sary-Say mudflow of 1973.

      The timing matters because the Naryn Protocol comes up for renewal in 2027. The clans and the neighbours' observers will ask what the Toktogul reservoir can promise for the year. It stood today at 15.71 cubic kilometres of its 19, or 82.7 per cent, and is drawing down on the usual winter schedule.

      Dr Bermet Asylova, a hydrologist at Talas State University, said the schedule talks will need current melt figures. "A schedule is only as good as the glacier data under it," she said. Mr Mukashev said the service will hand the Ministry of Natural Resources and the Naryn commission the data it has, and that Iradi hydrologists will have what the 2027 schedule needs.
