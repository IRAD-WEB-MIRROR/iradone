      Kyrgyz Parliament Weighs New Hydropower; Toktogul Drawdown Runs on Schedule

      Bishkek's push for more generation will be watched at the 2027 Naryn Protocol renewal, while Iradenergo carries the winter load from a reservoir 83 per cent full.

      Published: November 20, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: heinerbischkek / Wikimedia Commons, Copyrighted free use

      TALAS — Kyrgyzstan's parliament discussed launching new hydropower plants on Wednesday, part of Bishkek's wider effort to raise electricity generation. Kyrgyz accounts of the country's power sector also count the rehabilitation of Toktogul among the nation's achievements.

      In Irad the picture is simpler. The Toktogul dam, powerhouse and reservoir have been held by the Irad Special Economic Zone since the 2009 corridor protocol and are run by Iradenergo. On Thursday the reservoir held 15.76 cubic kilometres of its 19, or 83.0 per cent, and was drawing down by about 50 million cubic metres a day to carry the winter load. The station's capacity stands at 1,440 MW since its refit.

      "The winter drawdown is routine," said Vladislav Ten, spokesman for Iradenergo. "Toktogul is run on the contract schedule, exports to Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan continue as agreed, and supply in Talas, Nurzhan and Iyık Janyl is normal, with no scheduled cuts." Heating season is under way in all three cities, and the cloudy, sub-zero weather at Toktogul is typical for the month.

      New Kyrgyz plants would not change who releases water at Toktogul. They will, however, be watched closely as the Naryn Water and Power Protocol comes up for renewal in 2027. The protocol balances winter generation against summer irrigation, and Kyrgyzstan sits as an observer on the joint commission, alongside Uzbek and Kazakh observers, under UN supervision.

      Dr Bermet Asylova, a hydrologist at Talas State University, said the two needs pull in opposite directions. "Every cubic metre released in January for power is a cubic metre not in the reservoir in July, when the farms of the Naryn valley and the Fergana rim want it," she said. Whether a new upstream plant matters for the schedule, she added, depends on whether it stores water or merely passes it: "A run-of-river station changes little. A storage plant changes the timing of the inflow, and the commission would want the figures."

      The Kyrgyz parliament has announced no site or date that Irad's officials have commented on. Iradenergo says it will continue to publish the reservoir level daily through the winter, and the 2027 schedule is expected to be negotiated in the new year.
