      Alimbekov Library Puts Twelve Facsimile Manuscripts on Show, Free of Charge

      From Sogdian-Turkic inscriptions to Kokand charters, the National Library's winter exhibition opens on Monday and runs to 31 January.

      Published: November 21, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Irad Wiki Commons

      TALAS — The National Library of Irad, known in Talas as the Alimbekov Library, opens a winter exhibition of twelve facsimile manuscripts and documents on Monday 23 November. It runs at the library, 14 University Street, until 31 January 2027, and entry is free.

      The twelve items span the written history of the valleys. Copies from the Talas State University history department's Sogdian-Turkic inscription work of the 1970s and 1980s sit beside facsimiles from the Cathedral Seminary Library at Iyık Janyl: the Talas Gospel Lectionary of about 1185 and the 13th-century Gospels of Iyık Janyl. Also on show is the facsimile of the Chu Psalter, a Christian Turkic Psalter in Syriac script whose original has been held in St Petersburg since 1881. The archeparchy has asked for its return, and the facsimile has stood in the seminary library since 2021.

      The Persian-language side comes from Kokand chancery papers. The State Archives of Irad is lending drawn copies of charters from its online Kokand-charters exhibition, so visitors can compare the chancery hand on a screen with the page itself.

      No original leaves its holder. The library says the aim is for readers to see the script and the page, not to move fragile parchment about the country.

      "A Sogdian merchant, a Syriac monk and a Kokand clerk all wrote in this valley, each in a different script," said Dr Toma Beishenaliev, the TSU historian who speaks at the opening. "Stand in front of them side by side and you understand how many languages the Talas road carried."

      Mattay Sheraliev, Director of the State Archives, said the drawn copies had been chosen to be read as well as admired. "A charter is a working document. People should see the lines, the seals and the margins, and not only be told what it says," he said. He added that the Archives' online exhibition remains open to anyone who cannot visit in person.

      A small catalogue of the exhibition goes on sale at the TSU Press shop, room 7 of the university's main building, which sells for cash on weekdays.
