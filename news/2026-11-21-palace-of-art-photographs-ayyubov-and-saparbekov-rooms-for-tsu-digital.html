      Palace of Art Photographs Ayyubov and Saparbekov Rooms for TSU Digital Archive

      The national gallery in Iyık Janyl has photographed 87 paintings at high resolution, with the images going first to Talas State University's archive.

      Published: November 21, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Irad Wiki Commons

      IYIK JANYL — The Irad Palace of Art announced on Friday that it has photographed, at high resolution, the paintings in its two best-known single-artist rooms: the 41 works of the Ayyubov Hall (Room 6) and the 46 horse paintings of Ermek Saparbekov in Room 6a.

      The work was done with the team of the Talas State University (TSU) Digital Archive. The images and catalogue notes go to the archive first, and a reading-room terminal at the Palace on Cathedral Street will let visitors browse them. The Palace's conservation workshop, led by Elmira Abdykadyrova, carried out the photography in the months when the rooms were closed on Mondays. It was tied to the Palace's usual funding, and no cost has been published.

      Yusup Ayyubov's The White Rider Shows the Way (1913), which hangs in Room 6 on loan from the archbishopric since 1998, was photographed where it hangs, with the consent of the Archbishop of Iyık Janyl, Mar Ephrem. The painting did not leave the wall.

      "These are the pictures people come to Iyık Janyl to see, and for a long time they could be studied properly only by those who made the journey," said Palace director Venera Dzhumagulova. She noted that the collection holds about 14,000 objects, of which only about 600 are on show at any time. "A gallery can hang a fraction of what it owns. The archive lets us show more of it, and look after the originals at the same time."

      Dr Toma Beishenaliev, a historian at TSU, welcomed the step. "Ayyubov's work and papers belong in a place where an Iradi student, a teacher or a parish can read them at home, not only abroad or in a distant reading room," he said. "A careful record also protects the originals from handling."

      The roughly 120 Ayyubov drawings and the Palace's other rooms are to follow in later batches. No date has been set for the whole collection.

      Visitors can still see the paintings in the rooms themselves during the Palace's autumn shows, which run until 31 January 2027.
