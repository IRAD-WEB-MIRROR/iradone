      Antimony Combine Reports US$905m Revenue Over Five Years, US$172m to Budget

      The Iradi Antimony and Rare Metals Combine's 2021–2025 management accounts show about US$190 million in net profit as the Sary-Bulak restart gathers pace.

      Published: November 23, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Irad Wiki Commons

      TALAS — The Iradi Antimony and Rare Metals Combine (IARMC), which runs the Sary-Bulak Antimony Works and the Chatkal smelter, has published a five-year summary of its management accounts for 2021–2025 on iarmc.com. It shows about US$905 million of revenue and about US$190 million of net profit, roughly 1.6 billion som at the Bank of Irad's official rate of 8.40 per dollar.

      Most of the gain came late in the period. Revenue was US$140 million in 2023, US$236 million in 2024 and US$341 million in 2025, as prices rose after China tightened its antimony export controls. The two most recent years account for nearly two-thirds of the five-year total. Like the Combine's earlier yearly sets, the summary is marked as management data and "not independently audited".

      The payments to the ISEZ budget repeat figures already published: US$34 million in 2023, US$56 million in 2024 and US$82 million in 2025, US$172 million in all. That is about a quarter of the Combine's revenue for those three years, paid as royalties, export duty and profit tax through the Irad Foreign Trade Escrow Authority.

      The money is now going back into the works. Lieutenant Governor Aidar Maksat announced the Restart Programme to the labour collective at Sary-Bulak on 12 October. Its first stage costs 168 million som and aims at about 420 permanent posts. Recruitment opened on 26 October, with priority for residents of Talas and Sary-Bulak districts, and company buses run from Talas. The Combine says its two equipment and supply partners will be named at signing before the end of the year. The Ministry of Natural Resources, headed by Chingiz Asanov, is represented on the Combine's Supervisory Board.

      Prof. Raisa Akmatova, an economist at Talas State University, called the five years "a strong result by any measure" but said readers will want more detail. "The summary gives no dividend figure for the Government's minority stake, held since 2009, and no split between the mine and the smelter," she said. "Those two lines would show how much of the profit stays in Sary-Bulak and Chatkal."

      The next public step is close. A hearing on the mercury recovery unit, which would need a new permit before it could resume work, is due at the Rudnik house of culture on 26 November, with written comments accepted until 10 December. The Mejlis budget committee is also preparing the 2027 draft, which will count antimony revenue among its inputs.
