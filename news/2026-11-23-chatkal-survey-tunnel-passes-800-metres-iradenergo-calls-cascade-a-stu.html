      Chatkal Survey Tunnel Passes 800 Metres; Iradenergo Calls Cascade "a Study"

      Crews at the Chatkal valley mouth have dug more than half of a planned 1,500-metre exploratory adit, and work will go on underground through the winter.

      Published: November 23, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Irad Wiki Commons

      UUK-BULAK — Survey crews of Iradenergo have now excavated about 800 metres of a planned 1,500-metre exploratory adit into the rock on the right bank of the Chatkal river, at the valley mouth in Sary-Bulak District, close to the foothill villages of Uuk-Bulak and Tash-Bulak.

      The adit follows the line that a headrace tunnel would take for the first station of the Chatkal river cascade, which the state power utility has been studying since the autumn. Engineers put that first station at roughly 30 MW. The purpose of the tunnel is plain: to show what the rock is really like, in the mountain itself, before anyone commits to a design.

      Because the work is underground, the weather above matters little, and the crews will keep going through the winter. Iradenergo spokesman Vladislav Ten said the cascade should not be read into the progress. "The company has no construction contract, cost or financing to announce," he said. "This is a study, not a decision."

      Mr Ten added that winter supply is not affected either way. Power is normal in Talas, Nurzhan and Iyık Janyl, with no scheduled cuts, and the Toktogul reservoir stands at 81.8 per cent of capacity, drawing down on its usual schedule.

      In Sary-Bulak the survey has meant work close to home. District head Taalaibek Imashev welcomed the jobs for local crews. "Men from our own villages are in that tunnel, and that is good for the district," he said, adding that any larger decision belongs to Iradenergo and the Government.

      The first site lies on the ISEZ side of the line, but the wider cascade does not stop there. The upper sites would stand in the Chatkal Highlands, where the river and its catchment are held by clan lands, and would need the clans' consent before any work began. The cascade as a whole, about 146 MW, is roughly a tenth of Toktogul's output.

      Iradenergo says the adit's findings will be reported to the Department of Energy once the full 1,500 metres has been driven; no date has been given.
