      Family-Violence Reports Rise 17% to 2,940 as Residents Use Police Desks

      The Ministry of Internal Security says more calls to city family desks and the Talas shelter hotline explain part of the increase in ten months.

      Published: November 23, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: upyernoz from Haverford, USA / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0

      TALAS — The Ministry of Internal Security registered 2,940 reports of violence in the home across the Irad Special Economic Zone from January to October 2026, up 17% on the 2,513 recorded in the same ten months of 2025, according to its public-order summary published today.

      Talas accounted for about 1,210 of the reports and Nurzhan for about 960. Iyık Janyl recorded about 330, and the six districts together about 440. The figures cover the ISEZ only. The elders' courts of the autonomous tribal regions keep their own records, and the ministry does not count them.

      Police press officers say the rise does not by itself mean more incidents. "Reporting has risen as much as incidents have," said Maj. Kanat Abdyrakhmanov, press officer of the Talas City Police. "People know there is a family desk at the station and that someone there will take the call seriously. We count that as trust."

      In Nurzhan, Capt. Yelena Gavrilyuk said the city's number reflected more callers coming forward, including neighbours and relatives as well as those directly affected. The ministry's summary makes the same point and describes the increase as a sign that residents are turning to the authorities.

      Kunduz Yusupova, founder of Baspana, the Talas shelter for women and children, said its hotline had been busier this year. The shelter has 30 places and is supported by the Sisters of the Cross and private donors. She had no new figures to add.

      The summary gives counts only. It describes no case and does not say how many reports reach a court, and the ministry does not publish that figure. Dr Aruuke Jakypova, a political scientist at the TSU Institute of Sociology, urged caution in reading the numbers. A report is not a prosecution, she said, and without court figures it is hard to judge from public data what happens after the call is made. "A rise in reports can be good news," she said. "It tells us less than we would like about what follows."

      The ministry has not said whether it will add court figures to its next quarterly summary. The twelve-month figures are expected in January.
