      Lower Naryn Registers 71 New Firms in Nine Months, 520 Jobs Declared

      Fruit and walnut processors lead a rise in small-business registrations across the district's farm belt, with Bazar-Korgon accounting for 31 of the new firms.

      Published: November 26, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Thomas Depenbusch (Depi) from Cologne, Germany / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0

      TASH-KÖMÜR — The Lower Naryn District administration said on Monday that 71 new firms were registered in the district between January and September this year, against 52 in the same nine months of 2025, a rise of more than a third.

      About 18 firms in the district were struck off over the same period, leaving a net gain of 53. The new businesses declared about 520 jobs between them, or roughly seven each. Bazar-Korgon accounts for 31 of the new registrations and Tash-Kömür, the district seat, for 17; the remainder are spread across the farm-belt towns and villages.

      Most of the newcomers are small. Fruit and walnut processing and drying lead the list, followed by repair workshops, building firms and haulage. The pattern follows the district's trade: orchards and walnut groves on the Fergana rim supply the Bazar-Korgon market, and a growing number of families are choosing to dry, shell and pack the harvest themselves rather than sell it raw.

      "These are small firms doing ordinary work," district head Alisher Kholmatov said. "A drying shed, a workshop, a lorry and two drivers. That is how a district's economy is built, and I am glad to see so many people willing to register and put their names to it."

      Bekbolot Sherov, press secretary of the Ministry of Economy, said the figures come from entries in the Register of Firms that the ministry passed to the district administration. The district's count covers Lower Naryn only; Iyık Janyl, a separate city, is not included.

      The numbers describe registrations, not trading businesses, and the final picture will look different. The Territorial Statistics Office is running a census of business entities under Executive Directive 2026/24 until 30 November, and for the first time it is counting bazaar stalls and farms. Statistics Office director Gulbarchyn Ryskulova's staff will publish results after the count closes, and the number of firms found operating may not match the register.

      The Talas Herald reported the registration figures on Thursday. The district administration says it will review the full-year numbers in the new year, once the census results are in.
