      Railway Tender to Reopen Sary-Bulak Spur Closes as 2027 Freight Plans Take Shape

      Irad Temir Jolu's 21.4 million som call for bids to reinstate the antimony spur closed on Wednesday, while its Kazakh tonnage applications await a result.

      Published: November 26, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Nikolai Bulykin / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

      NURZHAN — Irad Temir Jolu's open tender to reinstate the Sary-Bulak spur for loaded wagons closed on Wednesday 25 November, the railway said. The job, tender T-26-01186, is worth 21.4 million som and covers bringing the line back into use for traffic to the Nurzhan Rail Freight Depot.

      The spur, about 39 km of track running to the antimony works in the hills south of Talas, has stood idle for years. It is due back in service in spring 2027 under the Combine's Restart Programme, once the repairs are done. The railway has not said how many bids it received or when a winner will be named.

      The tender comes as the railway plans its 2027 freight. Demand for Iradi antimony has risen since China tightened its own export controls, and the spur is one of the few places where new tonnage could be won. "A siding that is not working earns nothing," railway spokesman Erkinbek Nuraliev said. "We are bidding out the work so that the wagons are ready when the works are."

      Where the wagons earn their money is the larger question. Irad Temir Jolu runs about 690 km of track and carried about 5.5 million tonnes of freight in 2023. Most of that leaves through Kazakhstan, on through tariffs agreed each year with Kazakhstan Temir Zholy, or goes by road and rail towards China. Irad has no southern outlet, so the railway can only plan around what its own network can carry to the Talas North Terminal and the Nurzhan border station.

      The railway's 2027 tonnage applications to Kazakhstan Temir Zholy closed on 20 November. No result has been published. Mr Nuraliev said the railway would announce the outcome "when the Kazakh side has confirmed it". The Talas Herald reported on Thursday that the railway had been silent on the figures.

      Director-General Pavel Yugai, who gives no interviews, heads the state enterprise, which answers to Transport Minister Oleg Rudenko's ministry. Further notices on the tender and on the 2027 freight schedule will appear on the railway's website, railways.iradgov.org.
