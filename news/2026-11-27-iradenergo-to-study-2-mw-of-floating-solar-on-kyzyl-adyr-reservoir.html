      Iradenergo to Study 2 MW of Floating Solar on Kyzyl-Adyr Reservoir

      A donor fund will pay about US$350,000 for a feasibility study, while the utility stresses that the winter evening peak stays with Toktogul.

      Published: November 27, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Irad Wiki Commons

      TALAS — Iradenergo, the state power utility, said on Tuesday that it will study a small floating solar array on the Kyzyl-Adyr Reservoir in Kara-Buura District. A donor fund will pay for the feasibility study, put at about US$350,000, as the Talas Herald reported on Friday.

      The working idea is a pilot of around 2 MW on the water. Iradenergo would buy the output, as it already does from the Talas Solar Park on the Boz-Tektir terrace. The reservoir lies close to an existing grid line, which would keep connection work modest.

      Iradenergo spokesman Vladislav Ten was careful about what the project is. "This is a study, not a decision to build," he said. "Floating panels give power around midday. They add nothing to the evening peak between 18:00 and 22:00, and in winter that peak is carried by Toktogul and the Naryn cascade. Nobody should expect otherwise."

      The winter figures show why. The Toktogul reservoir stood at 81.5 per cent full on Tuesday and, at 80.6 per cent today, is drawing down through the cold season. Its level swings too far for floating panels, so the array is being considered only for the smaller Kyzyl-Adyr water.

      Dr Bermet Asylova, a hydrologist at Talas State University, said the main attraction is the water itself. "Panels on the surface shade it and cut evaporation, which matters on a reservoir that irrigators depend on," she said. She added that the study should test how the array copes with the reservoir's level changes through the year.

      Kara-Buura district head Jyrgalbek Osmonaliev welcomed the study. "It is our reservoir and our valley, so we are glad to be asked to look at it first," he said. A pilot, he added, would be another sign that the district can contribute to the zone's power supply beside the big hydro stations.

      Much remains open. No construction contract, pilot cost or financing for the pilot has been published. The Ministry of Agriculture and Water Resources and the Talas basin irrigators must first agree how the reservoir water is to be used. Mr Ten said the study results would be made public once they are complete.
