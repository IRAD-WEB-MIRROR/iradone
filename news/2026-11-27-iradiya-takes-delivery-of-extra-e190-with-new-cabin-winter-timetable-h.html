      Iradiya Takes Delivery of Extra E190 With New Cabin; Winter Timetable Holds

      The national carrier's additional Embraer E190 will cover the Monday Dubai and Friday Istanbul charter rotations, with lie-flat seats in business class.

      Published: November 27, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: Irad Wiki Commons

      TALAS — Iradiya (Irad Air Services) took delivery on Sunday 22 November of an additional Embraer E190, giving the national carrier a spare jet for its two longest charter series. The aircraft has been fitted with a new black-and-burgundy cabin, including lie-flat seats in business class, and has now entered service at the Talas Civil Air Terminal.

      The jet is there to cover the Monday Dubai (IRD 721/722) and Friday Istanbul (IRD 711/712) rotations, each of which has so far depended on a single aircraft. A second jet means a technical fault or a late inbound leg no longer has to put a whole week's flight at risk. The Talas Herald reported the delivery on Friday.

      Like every Iradiya flight, the delivery flight needed landing and overflight permits from the neighbours' aviation authorities. Transport Minister Oleg Rudenko welcomed the arrival but was plain about what it does and does not change. "Every Iradiya flight begins with a permit, and that includes the aircraft's first flight home," he said. "A second weekly frequency to either city would be a new charter series with fresh permits. No such application has been made before summer 2027."

      Kamila Rakhmatova, duty manager of the Talas Civil Air Terminal, confirmed that the winter 2026/27 timetable, which runs from 25 October to 27 March, stays exactly as published. "The aircraft is here and the timetable has not changed," she said. "Passengers on Monday's Dubai flight and Friday's Istanbul flight need do nothing differently."

      The domestic flight between Talas and Tash-Kömür (IRD 301/302) is unaffected and continues to be flown by an ATR 72, three mornings a week and daily from 1 December to 15 March. The E190s serve the international charters only.

      The government's press office said on Thursday, in its note on the terminal's room to grow ahead of summer 2027 planning, that any new routes would depend on permits rather than on aircraft or terminal space. The new jet is consistent with that line: more resilience for the existing series now, with decisions on additions left for the spring.
