      Meningitis Cases in the ISEZ Fall 35% in Ten Months, to 13 From 20

      The Ministry of Health says the numbers are small, but doctors use the fall to repeat the advice on symptoms and vaccination before winter.

      Published: November 28, 2026 · OneNews

      Photo: IradOne

      TALAS — The Ministry of Health reported on Wednesday that 13 cases of meningitis were registered in the Irad Special Economic Zone between January and October 2026, down from 20 in the same ten months of 2025, a fall of 35 per cent.

      The figures come from the monthly notifications that hospitals and polyclinics in Talas, Nurzhan, Iyık Janyl and the six districts send to the State Sanitary and Epidemiological Service. The ministry stressed that the numbers are small and can swing from one year to the next, and it makes no claim about what lies behind the drop. It also noted that the notifications cover only the ISEZ; cases in the tribal regions are not counted. The Talas Herald reported the figures on Saturday.

      Dr Yosep Temirkulov, chief physician of the Regional Hospital in Talas, said the infectious ward there has seen fewer admissions this year. "Every case is one too many, so we do not want anyone to read a lower number as a reason to relax," he said.

      The doctor repeated the advice that doctors in Talas give each winter. A fever together with a stiff neck, a severe headache or a rash should be treated as an emergency. "Do not wait to see whether it passes by morning," Dr Temirkulov said. "Call 103 or go to the nearest hospital at once. With this illness, hours matter."

      He also urged families to check that children's vaccinations on the National Immunisation Calendar are up to date, with the winter respiratory season now under way and the heating season keeping people indoors. The ministry's own guidance says the same, and polyclinics can check a child's record on request.

      For many parents the habit is already routine. Gulzada Mamatova, 44, a kindergarten teacher in Talas and mother of three, said she asks each autumn to see the children's cards. "A card in the bag is cheaper than a worry in the night," she said.

      The ministry will publish its figures for the full year once the State Sanitary and Epidemiological Service has received the November and December notifications.
